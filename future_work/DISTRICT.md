# Indian district crop data, prices and irrigation

Pmjarvis reconstructed the historical district study from ICRISAT and Tata-Cornell Institute (TCI) exports. The main model covers 176 of the 311 historical districts from agricultural year 1966–67 through 2017–18. Another 1998–2017 model and two matched comparisons show what changes with the study period, crop prices and irrigation coverage. The archived editions and all analytical choices are identified below. The current live edition remains unconfirmed. [Findings and figures](FINDINGS.md#official-indian-district-data) · [Download and access record](results/district_status.json)

## Which districts have data?

All **311 historical parent districts** in the crop export have some positive reported production, matching map geometry and a complete 52-year climate series. The crop export itself contains **16,146 of 16,172** possible district-year records. A district can have a row in a given year even when several crop or price fields in that row are missing. These four exclusion reasons are mutually exclusive within each column. The two long-window districts below the priced-value threshold are Karbi Anglong and Nagaon in Assam. In the shorter window, the trend prevents an index for Ranchi and Santhal Paragana / Dumka in Jharkhand. For the long-window main model, all the historical districts in Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Kerala and Odisha are excluded even though the crop export contains production from each state. The exclusion table gives every omitted district by name, state, window and reason. [Named exclusions](results/district_exclusions.csv) · [Regression sample sizes](results/district_comparison.csv)

**Modern India has districts without separate agricultural records in this project.** The presented government production table contains 739 district codes with some positive output in 1998–2022. The government irrigation export contains 741 nonmissing district codes at some point in 1998–2023, 738 of which have at least one reported area. Those counts combine years with different boundaries. A later district can appear inside its parent’s earlier total. For example, Arunachal Pradesh’s **Bichom and Keyi Panyor** have no separate entry in the presented crop or supplemental irrigation tables. The state gazette documents their creation in 2024. Arunachal Pradesh also falls outside the 20-state historical crop export. A complete present-day count of missing districts requires a dated national roster and a verified correspondence through splits and mergers. This study establishes named gaps and reports each source’s own coverage. ## What enters a district estimate

- **Crop identity and units.** The input retains 19 individual crops with both area and production. Annual sorghum already includes its seasons. Aggregate oilseeds, mixed minor pulses and area-only series are excluded. Fifteen crops have matching farm-price fields. Farm prices are multiplied by 10 to obtain rupees per metric tonne. The reported area and production are multiplied by 1,000 to obtain hectares and tonnes. Publisher spellings for sesame and mustard are mapped explicitly. The codebook covers every item in the crop export. [Commodity crosswalk](results/district_crop_definitions.csv)
- **Processing stage.** The ICRISAT manual identifies rice production as milled rice, sugarcane production as gur (raw sugar), and cotton production as lint. Only the rice price can value rice and only the gur price can value gur. The publisher had already converted the relevant production series. Raw seed-cotton (kapas) prices and paddy prices cannot value the corresponding lint or milled tonnage here. Soyabean, sunflower, safflower and cotton can contribute area to diversity, while their production receives no farm price. [Publisher manual in the World Bank deposit, printed pp. 10 and 17](https://reproducibility.worldbank.org/catalog/409/study-description)
- **Missingness and precision.** The official missing code is −1. Reported zero production is retained. A zero price cannot value production. In 837 crop-years, positive production accompanies an area reported as zero. The publisher says a zero can represent less than 500 ha. Both the reported area and tonnage are preserved. The analysis leaves the area, yield and irrigation share missing for those rows. It makes no estimate of their hectares. [Normalized crop-year records and flags](results/district_crop_year.parquet)
- **Fixed prices and coverage.** Each crop’s annual tonnes are multiplied by that district’s observed 2015 price, the middle year of the presented 2014–16 reference period. The main index needs at least three positively producing crops with valid areas and priced output in every year. These balanced crops must account for at least 70% of the value of crops that could be priced. The median eligible district’s balanced crops cover **56.35%** of reported hectares in the 19-crop universe over the long window. Prices for crops of minor local importance are often missing. Following a split, the publisher kept the price observed in the truncated parent district. It may represent the larger area imperfectly. [District coverage for both windows](results/district_coverage.csv) · [Exclusions](results/district_exclusions.csv)
- **Irrigation and conflicting records.** The primary adjustment uses last year’s irrigated hectares divided by last year’s area across the same balanced output crops. Every positive-area crop needs a usable irrigation observation. The crop-year export flags 9,117 records with irrigated area above reported crop area. The manual describes crop irrigation as unavailable in West Bengal and in Maharashtra after 2002, and farm prices as unavailable in Odisha (source name Orissa) from 1988 onward. The primary estimates follow these restrictions and preserve the reported values in separate fields. A second set of fits uses the numbers as printed wherever they are physically admissible, retaining the same checks on units and irrigation bounds. [Manual sensitivity](results/district_manual_sensitivity.csv) · [Its underlying district-years](results/district_manual_sensitivity_panel.parquet)
- **Exposure and model.** December SPEI-12 measures moisture balance over the preceding 12 months. The pipeline intersects half-degree grid cells with the official district shapes and weights them by their share of the fixed MapSPAM cropland footprint. At least 90% of cropland weight must have observed climate. A fixed modern footprint is an assumption for historical cropland. All 311 modelled geometries have climate throughout the window. The outcome is the annual proportional deviation of fixed-price output from its district LOWESS trend. Hill-1 is the effective number of crops from reported areas in the preceding five years, standardized across the full estimation sample for each window. A district needs five prior years before contributing an outcome to the regression. The restricted and adjusted fits keep the same standardization and the same rows. District and state-by-year fixed effects absorb persistent district differences and shocks shared within a state-year. Confidence intervals use district-clustered standard errors. [District climate](results/district_climate.parquet) · [1966–2017 panel](results/district_panel.parquet) · [1998–2017 panel](results/district_panel_overlap.parquet)

The full archive and later-window coefficients measure associations in eligible districts. Switching datasets changes geography, priced crops, sample years and sometimes the districts. To isolate the effect of price weights, a separate analysis holds the districts, years, drought, diversity and positively producing crop baskets fixed. It compares 2014, 2015 and 2016 district prices with the presented FAOSTAT national price schedule. All four schedules need the same matched crop, three or more complete crops and at least 70% coverage of the original 2015 priced value. Gur is excluded because the presented comparator prices cane. Each monetary series is compared through its own proportional output anomaly. [Price schedules by district and crop](results/district_price_basket.csv) · [Matched annual outputs](results/district_price_panel.parquet) · [Price estimates](results/district_price_sensitivity.csv)

## What can be compared across district boundaries

The publisher apportions later districts back to 1966 parents. Frozen DES and five-crop data use modern district identifiers. Equal-looking codes in these systems do not establish a match. The comparison requires a unique state and district name after punctuation, case and the Orissa/Odisha rename are normalized, membership in the presented DES core with a matched polygon, and no parent or child appearance in the 332 recorded allocations. It then calculates polygon intersection divided by union in equal-area EPSG:6933. The rule includes **27** districts at 80% overlap, **15** at 85%, **two** at 90% and **zero** at 95%. No pair qualifies as an identical polygon. The cutoffs are sensitivity choices. Imperfect digitization, historical transfers and boundary changes outside the split history can contribute to differences. Every district’s overlap and selection flags are exported. [Boundary audit](results/district_boundary_comparison.csv) · [Agreement at stricter thresholds](results/district_boundary_sensitivity.csv)

The physical comparison joins common crops and years on those 27 units. It reports hectares or tonnes and preserves both values and their signed difference (archived minus comparator). Cane and gur tonnes cannot be paired. Cotton area can be compared, but the frozen DES table lacks a district-row bale/tonne indicator and national DES cotton statistics use 170 kg bales. Cotton tonnage is therefore excluded from the cross-source comparison. Seasonal land-use rows require every expected seasonal component. A blank never becomes a zero. Rice and sorghum have no jointly usable annual government irrigation totals under this complete-season rule in the aligned sample. For the government irrigation comparison, the modern district code **and** canonical district and state names must agree. In the agreement chart the denominator is pairs reported on both sides with at least one positive value. Its tolerance is the larger of 10 hectares or tonnes and 1% of the comparator. Exact counts, both-zero counts, missing values, and absolute differences are available separately. [Paired measurements](results/district_paired_quantities.parquet) · [Agreement by crop and unit](results/district_quantity_agreement.csv) · [Government cotton units, table 2.19](https://www.nccd.gov.in/uploads/Agricultural_Statisitcs_at_a_Glance_2024_25_11da3cd37d.pdf)

The two agricultural publications can share upstream government records. Agreement measures the consistency of the released series. For Jaisalmer chickpea in 2013–14, the apportioned table records zero irrigated hectares and the DES land-use record gives 141,976 ha. Both source values are preserved. Their underlying cause is unknown. Even exact agreement between archived copies cannot establish field-level accuracy.

## Reproduce and verify

From the repository root, using the environment in the [extension setup](README.md#reproduction), run the following commands.

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m future_work.district_land_use
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m future_work india
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m future_work verify
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m pytest -q future_work/tests
```

The first command checks the government datastore against the CSV. The CSV’s records are ordered differently from the API. The check uses the unique published record ID, exact text fields and decimal-valued numbers. It preserves the distinction between missing area and zero. The receipt records separate checksums for the original page responses and normalized observations. Every district rebuild checks the CSV against the complete normalized page inventory. [Full datastore audit](district_land_use.py) · [61 page receipts](district_land_use_pages.csv)

The default district run retrieves six pinned files under `future_work/local/raw/icrisat_archive/`, checks their digests, processes the original portal JSON, reconciles the World Bank deposit and compares the DES land-use download. Downloaded bytes stay in the local cache. Reuse that cache for an offline rebuild. The live availability probes are informational. A failed acquisition or source check stops before publishing new analysis tables. Verification independently reconstructs balanced crop values, lagged diversity and irrigation, and refits the district coefficients and clustered intervals. When the raw files are cached it also compares native observations and checks climate by intersecting cells directly for three contrasting districts and years. [Pipeline](district_archive.py) · [Source comparisons](district_comparison.py) · [Independent checks](district_validation.py) · [Output digests](outputs.csv)

If a later official edition becomes available, put its original files and a `release.json` under `future_work/local/raw/icrisat/`. Set `series` to `apportioned`, identify `release_id`, `boundary_vintage`, `analysis_years` and `price_reference_year`, and supply a saved publisher `evidence` file with its `latest_evidence_quote`. Describe the `apy`, `prices`, `irrigation` and `geography` files with filename, original publisher URL, SHA-256, boundary vintage and source keys. Provide field names, crop labels, units and missing-value codes from that edition. Geography can use original polygons or an official child membership crosswalk. With an archived receipt present the command publishes the registered edition’s tables with a separate `registered_` prefix. [Registered-release reader](district.py) · [Tested manifest example](tests/test_methods.py)

A future explorer revision would need a release selector, parent-district identifiers and geometry, crop product form, price-basis label, data-coverage fields and access to the model sample and boundary diagnostics.
