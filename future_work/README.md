## Reproduction

Use the project's Python 3.11 environment and the frozen raw inputs or the local restoration snapshot. The extension adds the Blosc decoder for hourly ERA5 and the Excel reader for official `.xlsx` district exports. With the environment active, install the [extension requirement](requirements.txt). The example uses the existing environment and places the extra package only in the extension's ignored local directory.

```sh
uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python --target future_work/local/python --no-deps -r future_work/requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python -m future_work verify
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m future_work all
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m pytest -q future_work/tests
```

The same entry point accepts `season`, `heat`, `history`, `india`, and `report`. Tables go into `future_work/results`; plots go into `future_work/figures`. Large source downloads, cached daily temperatures and the restoration snapshot are local. Hourly acquisition resumes from complete days. Heat uses the previous year for crops harvested in 1993. The India stage rebuilds the archived district study, its three figures and the findings from pinned inputs. Its status record also reports current publisher access. [District source files](district_sources.json)

The hourly study reads roughly 41 GB of compressed source objects and saves compact daily excess-degree sums. The pinned ledger records each object’s generation and publisher MD5. Each saved day records its contributing sources, verifies downloaded bytes, and compares any earlier daily sums before replacing the cache. Four changing discovery pages are preserved in their [catalogue snapshot](source_catalogues.zip). They identify the exact Zenodo files and NOAA menus and weeks used. The [source digest inventory](source_files.csv.gz) also covers the calendar and wheat assets, monthly SPEI and NOAA responses. Historical FAOSTAT inputs, cropland weights and presented climate files are covered by the [freeze manifest](baseline.csv). The [output digest inventory](outputs.csv) permits independent checks of the tables and images without downloading any hourly files. `python -m future_work verify` rechecks both the presentation freeze and follow-up outputs. `python -m future_work.freeze verify-snapshot` checks all original copies, including the historical documentation.

## Growing-season drought

For each crop, grid cell and water regime, the GGCMI calendar provides a planting day and a maturity day on a 365-day template. The analysis chooses the published SPEIbase v2.10 index at harvest whose accumulation length covers every calendar month touched by the growing period. A cross-year season is assigned to the harvest year. Monthly drought data resolve whole months. Heat uses the actual calendar days, including February 29 when a season spans it. [GGCMI calendars](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5062513) · [SPEIbase v2.10](https://doi.org/10.20350/digitalCSIC/16497)

MapSPAM 2020 physical hectares distinguish rainfed and irrigated land. Published wheat areas allocate the two wheat calendars within each regime. The first and second rice calendars use their published shares, with the uncovered share counted as missing. Both millet classes use the published millet calendar. Broad mixed crop classes lacking a specific calendar are recorded in the scope table. The 2020 footprint and the static calendars are measurement assumptions throughout the analysis. [MapSPAM](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/SWPENT) · [Winter and spring wheat areas](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3773827) · [Crop coverage and exclusions](results/season_crop_scope.csv)

The half-degree grid uses the presented pipeline's country intersections and its January 1993 SPEI valid-cell template. This detail matters for Brazil. Each country-crop needs valid drought data on at least 90% of its rainfed plus irrigated hectares in each of the 31 years. The fixed comparison basket additionally needs both presented exposure series. In `season_crop_year.parquet`, `coverage` equals `observed_ha` divided by `regime_area_ha`, the sum of reported rainfed and irrigated hectares. `area_ha` supplies the national weight from the independently rounded all-regime raster. The two area totals can differ slightly for the same crop. The stored national December estimate, which covers all crops, supplies a fourth reference. Every table identifies its sample. [Country coverage](results/season_area_coverage.csv) · [Country-crop-year measurements](results/season_crop_year.parquet)

A drought year has exposure at or below −1. An isolated drought event has three prior drought-free years and a complete three-year follow-up. Resistance compares event-year output with the preceding three years. Recovery compares the following three years with the event year. Both use the detrended national output index. Crop response is the slope of relative yield anomaly on exposure for the same reported yield years in each comparison. Response diversity is the value-share-weighted standard deviation of those slopes. The loss model estimates the change in the national output anomaly in a drought year with country and year fixed effects, a drought-by-diversity interaction, and country-clustered uncertainty.

## Daily heat and observed vegetation

Extreme degree-days measure accumulated heat above a chosen temperature. For each UTC hour, take the positive difference between ERA5 two-metre air temperature and the threshold in °C, divide by 24, and sum over the crop season. Thresholds are 28, 30 and 32°C, with 30°C primary. They are common analytical thresholds across crops. Temperature is evaluated on a 1.5° grid, so local extremes at scales finer than that grid are smoothed. The half-degree crop cells are divided between overlapping temperature cells using spherical area. [ERA5](https://doi.org/10.24381/cds.adbb2d47) · [WeatherBench 2](https://weatherbench2.readthedocs.io/en/latest/data-guide.html) · [Public ARCO-ERA5 archive](https://github.com/google-research/arco-era5)

The WeatherBench hourly archive supplies 1992–2022. For 2023 the code applies the same spherical, area-conserving regridding to the corresponding native ERA5 hourly field. A real overlap check compares the two archives on every coarse-grid cell at three hours. Complete 24-hour daily periods are required. The source ledger pins the exact cloud generation and publisher checksum for 14,422 production chunks and the extra WeatherBench chunk used in the archive comparison. [Archive object ledger](era5_source_objects.csv.gz)

The Vegetation Condition Index (VCI) measures satellite greenness relative to historical conditions on a 0–100 scale. Higher values indicate greener conditions. NOAA publishes weekly country averages over identified crop areas under the operational `GC_current` version. The code accepts country and crop identifiers from NOAA's actual menu, treats the service's negative fill values as missing, and excludes composite territories absent from that menu. It uses each country's crop calendars to weight only weeks overlapping the crop season. NOAA's 52 seven-day composites cover days 1–364. The remaining one or two days have missing vegetation observations. A calendar component needs valid VCI on 90% of season days, and covered components must represent 90% of its calendar hectares. [NOAA crop-specific product](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/smcd/emb/vci/VH/vh_adminMeanByCrop.php?type=Country_Weekly_MeanPlot) · [NOAA VCI definition](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/smcd/emb/vci/VH/VH-Syst_10ap30.php)

The full-period satellite series is a country-crop average. NOAA's published crop means use its SPAM 2010 crop mask, while the heat analysis uses MapSPAM 2020 physical hectares. The country-crop VCI cannot resolve within-country pixel differences. The 1993–2023 basket contains calendar-complete crops with at least one observed satellite season. Each country retains that same basket in every year. A country-year enters the effect model only when every crop in its basket has a seasonal VCI. Archive-wide gaps affect 1994–1995 and 2004 especially. The heat trend uses all 31 temperature years. All models then use identical country-years for estimating output and vegetation differences. A hot year exceeds its country's fitted temperature trend by at least one residual standard deviation. A country with zero variation for any tested degree-day threshold cannot enter the common standardized comparison. Country and year fixed-effects models compare hot-only, dry-only and compound years with years meeting neither threshold. Output anomalies use total national crop value. Greenness differences are reported in VCI points.

For a spatial check, 260 weekly NOAA 16 km VCI rasters from 2019–2023 provide complete source weeks for harvests in 2020–2023. The satellite composite dates come from the files. Packed integers are scaled to physical VCI units. Flagged invalid or cold pixels are excluded and the land or coast flag is required. Each half-degree cell needs observations on at least half its area. Within a crop season, at least 90% of days and 90% of calendar hectares must qualify. The comparison uses identical crop-area weights and country-crop seasons across products, with four complete years per retained crop. The two measures share satellite observations and their agreement measures sensitivity to aggregation. [Gridded NOAA source](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/data/pub0018/VHPdata4users/data/Blended_VH_16km/VH/) · [Paired observations](results/vci_footprints_paired.parquet) · [Footprint agreement](results/vci_footprint_agreement.csv)

## Extreme drought over 1961–2023

The reader uses harvested area, production and constant 2014–2016 international-dollar gross production value from the original FAOSTAT bulk files. Every overlapping country, item and year is checked against the frozen table, including missingness, units and imputation flags. Missing values are filled only where the original pipeline can recover FAOSTAT's own constant item price. The territory ledger lists every contributing or excluded FAOSTAT area and the years for which it appears. The published Belgium–Luxembourg and Sudan groupings use their historical parent and successors without double counting. Former units without a supported correspondence are excluded. [FAOSTAT crop data](https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/QCL) · [FAOSTAT production value](https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/QV) · [Territory ledger](results/history_territories.csv)

The extended panel requires observations in every year, a mean harvested area of at least 100,000 hectares, five or more crops, and at least 90% observed climate coverage. At least 51 of 63 years must satisfy the presented annual reporting-quality rule. This preserves the original requirement of 25 good years out of 31 as a proportion. A good year has at most half its priced value flagged estimated or imputed. The time-invariant 2020 cropland footprint is used in both windows. [Country eligibility and reasons](results/history_eligibility.csv)

The severity model uses the original exclusive drought bins, country and year fixed effects, clustered uncertainty, and Hill-1 crop diversity standardized across countries in the estimation sample. Extreme means national December SPEI-12 ≤ −2. The country permutation test uses the presented seed and 2,000 shuffles of entire country diversity assignments. The detectable effect assumes a two-sided 5% test and 80% power using the observed clustered standard error and a noncentral t distribution. It is an approximate sensitivity measure conditional on the observed design and variance. Longer windows also change the data entering the diversity estimate and the LOWESS trend. [Estimates and uncertainty](results/history_severity.csv)

## Direct measurement checks

The [source audit](results/source_measurement_checks.json) recalculates seasonal SPEI for India wheat, Brazil soybean and Nigeria pearl millet directly from the corresponding monthly source rasters. It also integrates daily heat across four country-crop seasons by looping over each planting-to-harvest date and crop-area component. The Dominican Republic’s 2023 cotton harvest spans both ERA5 archives. It retrieves one full day of hourly temperatures directly from each ERA5 archive and compares all three daily thresholds on all 29,040 grid cells. The archive comparison has a separate receipt. A second model fit uses explicit country and year indicators and independently computes country-clustered standard errors. It checks coefficients, confidence intervals and p-values for each seasonal drought comparison, each heat and vegetation specification and the full historical severity model. The paired response figure is checked against the frozen yield anomalies, reported value shares and seven-year event windows for every plotted crop and country. ## Official Indian district data

Pmjarvis recovered the ICRISAT/TCI apportioned crop, farm-harvest price, irrigation and boundary exports. They support a 1966–2017 panel and a 1998–2017 comparison. All 1,210,950 numerical crop-table entries match the World Bank deposit. Current live-release status, missing prices, conflicts with the publisher manual and the historical/modern boundary differences are documented with the estimates.
